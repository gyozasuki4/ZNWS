Weather Operations

Detailed procedures for the ZASNet operational map, workstations, Hazard Services, warning criteria, hydro tools, product lifecycle controls, and administration.

Multi-purpose products · Operations standard

This suite communicates short-term forecasts, heightened awareness, reports, outlooks, and national-center discussions. None substitutes for a warning when warning criteria are met.

Product Purpose and timing Required operational content
Short Term Forecast (NOW) Non-scheduled forecast for the next six hours. Use plain, future-tense language. Do not update or amend a NOW; issue a new product when conditions change. Zone UGC for broad public dissemination or county UGC through WarnGen, affected area, conditions, evolution or movement, and timing. Specific sub-severe hail/wind magnitudes belong in an SPS.
Special Weather Statement (SPS) Ongoing or imminent hazards needing heightened awareness, normally within six hours. A significant event beyond six hours may expire in up to 12 hours. Impact-based convection uses basis/motion; HAZARD, SOURCE, and IMPACT; locations; CTA; a 3–20 point LAT…LON polygon; TIME…MOT…LOC; then optional LANDSPOUT/WATERSPOUT, MAX HAIL SIZE, and MAX WIND GUST tags in order.
Hazardous Weather Outlook (HWO) Potential hazardous weather through day seven for preparedness and decision support. Separate near-term hazards from later potential and convey uncertainty. Office area, days/timing, hazards and severity, confidence, spotter-activation statement when appropriate, and references to current warnings/outlooks. It does not replace a watch, warning, or advisory.
Local Storm Report (LSR) Prompt preliminary report of significant weather in the office area. Correct the report when better information arrives. Event type, date/time and time zone, location and distance/direction, county/state, latitude/longitude when available, magnitude and units, source, and concise remarks. Mark estimated values and protect private personal information.
SPC Mesoscale Discussion (MD) SPC-authored technical discussion of a focused severe-weather situation, including watch likelihood and reasoning. Local offices monitor and redistribute it without rewriting its conclusions. Discussion number, valid period, affected area, concerning line, probability/watch language, summary, technical discussion, and authoritative SPC polygon.

Impact-based SPS criteria

  • Use sub-severe wind/gust values from 40 through 57 mph and hail smaller than one inch. Warning-level conditions require the appropriate warning.
  • Frequent lightning, non-threatening landspouts/funnel clouds, and inland waterspouts may also be addressed.
  • Valid sources include radar, trained spotters, law enforcement, emergency management, broadcast media, and the public.
  • Standard maximum-wind tags are 40 or 50 mph; use less-than-40 only when hail is the qualifying hazard. Maximum hail is 0.88 inch.
  • Review HAZARD, SOURCE, IMPACT, locations, CTA, LAT…LON, TIME…MOT…LOC, and coded-tag order before issuance.

1. Starting an operational shift

Sign-in and office identity

  1. Sign in through Authentik and verify the name shown at the bottom of the tool rail.
  2. Open More → Ops settings. Confirm the three-letter active WFO. Every draft, warning identifier, map filter, and distribution record depends on this selection.
  3. Confirm whether Practice Mode is enabled. Practice products stay inside Ops and do not reach public pages, Mattermost, or the GR warning server.
  4. Enable Mutual Aid only when you are authorized to operate another office.

System readiness

  1. Open More → Data & server.
  2. Confirm the application server is online and review each feed’s freshness.
  3. Open Radar, select the intended site and product, and verify the latest scan time.
  4. Review active ZASNet products and existing external alerts over the CWA.
  5. Load a saved map setup or arrange the layers required for the expected event.

Before authoring

Check the issuing office, current warning inventory, event tracking numbers, data timestamps, and whether another operator holds an edit lock. Do not create a second product merely because an existing product is not visible in the current filtered view.

2. Workspaces and mutual aid

A workspace is an independent operating desk. It has its own WFO, map camera, radar site/product, panel layout, open tools, and Hazard Services draft. Issued products remain shared across all workspaces.

Opening another office

  1. Select the + button in the workspace row above the map.
  2. Enter the office’s three-letter ID, such as CYS, BIS, or OHX.
  3. Wait for the new tab to load its office boundary and preferred radar.
  4. Verify the WFO label before drawing or issuing anything.

How alerts behave

  • The map can display external and ZASNet products from surrounding offices regardless of which workspace is active.
  • Products authored from a workspace use that workspace’s operated office identity.
  • Edit and cancel actions are normally restricted to the issuing office. Mutual Aid permits authorized cross-office operations.
  • Edit locks prevent two operators from modifying the same active ZASNet product at once. A lock renews while editing and eventually expires if abandoned.

Good practice

Keep one office per tab. Name and save drafts clearly. Before switching tabs, confirm whether the current draft is saved; switching workspaces must never be treated as cancelling an issued product.

3. Map controls and panel layouts

Base map and navigation

  • CWA centers the active map on the selected office.
  • Locate requests the browser’s current location.
  • Pan by dragging, zoom with the wheel/pinch gesture, and use normal map rotation controls when needed.
  • Changing radar scans or products does not intentionally recenter the map.

Multiple panels

Choose Single, Dual, Quad, or Main + 3 in Map settings. Each panel has independent radar and layer state. When Link panel navigation is on, panning or zooming one panel moves the others. Turn it off when comparing separate offices or scales.

Saved setups

  1. Arrange the panel count, radar products, layers, and map views.
  2. Open Layers → Saved setup.
  3. Enter a descriptive name and select Save.
  4. Use Load to restore the setup. Use New before creating a separate configuration.

City labels

Density determines the population threshold and zoom behavior. Text size changes label scale. Very dense labels may be useful during warning placement but can obscure radar at regional scales.

4. Radar operations

Choosing a source

Site radar
Level 2 and Level 3 products from the selected radar site.
National MRMS
An animated national mosaic useful for broad situational awareness.
National IEM NEXRAD
A latest-frame national fallback.

Site products

  • Reflectivity shows returned power and is the primary precipitation/storm-structure view.
  • Velocity shows radial motion toward or away from the radar. Interpret it with range, beam height, aliasing, and storm motion in mind.
  • Level 3 products provide derived/site products and may arrive at different times than Level 2 scans.
  • Derived hail products are guidance and should not replace multi-source analysis.

Timeline and animation

  • The selected frame controls the displayed scan and the linked Hazard Services motion point.
  • A red frame means that frame failed or is unavailable. It is not a meteorological alert.
  • Verify the scan timestamp before using it for warning placement.
  • If the newest scan is stale, acknowledge the notice only after checking Data & Server and another source.

Inspect mode

Use radar inspect/query to view the value beneath the pointer. Readouts include product value, range from radar, azimuth/gate information where available, and the scan timestamp. Inspect mode is diagnostic; warning decisions still require environmental and observational context.

If a radar fails

  1. Select a previous valid frame.
  2. Try the alternate Level 2/Level 3 product.
  3. Use a neighboring site or national mosaic.
  4. Check feed health and the displayed timestamp.
  5. Do not present an old image as current.

5. Layers and situational awareness

Layers are grouped by purpose and can be searched with the layer search box. Use favorites for frequently toggled data. Drag active layers to change visual stacking and adjust opacity when overlapping products hide one another.

Operational boundaries

  • CWA boundaries show office responsibility.
  • County and public/fire/marine zone boundaries support product selection.
  • Roads, highways, cities, rivers, and hydrography provide geographic context.

Observations

  • Surface observations provide temperature, dew point, wind, visibility, ceiling, and flight category.
  • Storm reports provide preliminary event type, magnitude, location, time, and remarks.
  • River gauges show current NWPS flood category and observed value.
  • Observation timestamps must be evaluated before use.

Guidance and outlooks

  • SPC outlooks, watches, discussions, fire outlooks, and storm reports serve different purposes; an outlook is not a warning.
  • WPC precipitation products and MPDs provide heavy-rain context.
  • NHC official products must remain distinguishable from model guidance and ZASNet-authored tracks.
  • Smoke, air-quality, fire, drought, snow, lightning, and model layers are guidance unless explicitly labeled as an issued alert.

Alert rendering

Zone-based alerts use solid fills without outlines. Polygon products use their actual polygon rather than filling every county they touch. Administratively configured product priority determines which overlapping fill appears above another.

6. Hazard Services workflow

Selecting a product

Open HAZ or press H. Products are grouped by Convection, Marine, Flood, Winter, Coastal/Lakeshore, Fire Weather, and Non-precipitation. Select the exact product; similar colors or names do not imply interchangeable text or VTEC.

Step 1 — Validity

  • Choose NEW for a new event, or load an active product for CON, EXT, CAN, or EXP.
  • Confirm the effective and ending times in the operated office’s local time.
  • Use an operationally reasonable duration. The default is a starting point, not a forecast decision.

Step 2 — Geometry

Convective products use storm placement/tracking or a freehand polygon. Static polygon products use area drawing. Zone products use complete selectable zones.

Step 3 — Details

Only fields belonging to the selected hazard should appear. Values populate template tokens in WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, IMPACTS, ADDITIONAL DETAILS, tags, or narrative blocks. Blank optional fields are omitted unless the template explicitly supplies default wording.

Step 4 — Locations

Review every selected county, zone, marine area, city, and pathcast location. Automatic intersection is a starting point. Geometry determines UGC selection; manually editing narrative place names does not change the official area codes.

Step 5 — Review

Generate the product again after changing any field. Review is not a live word processor connected to form inputs: a newly entered value appears only after regeneration. Protected envelope sections cannot be changed manually.

Drafts

Save unfinished work before leaving the workflow. Resume restores the saved product and fields. Always regenerate Review after resuming so times and imported data are current.

7. Geometry and motion

Single storm

  1. Place the storm marker on the current cell.
  2. Move to an earlier radar frame and drag that frame’s linked dot onto the same cell.
  3. Move to a later frame and repeat.
  4. The fitted motion projects the threat corridor through expiration.
  5. Refine the yellow polygon handles without unnecessarily expanding the warning.

Line of storms

  1. Select Line as the storm shape.
  2. Place the two endpoint controls at the ends of the current line.
  3. Use linked center points on two or more frames to establish motion.
  4. Review the projected line width and downstream corridor.

Manual polygon controls

  • Enter or double-click finishes the initial polygon.
  • Drag yellow handles to refine it.
  • Delete removes the selected/recent point; right-clicking a handle removes that vertex.
  • Trim to CWA removes geometry outside the operated office when that is operationally correct.
  • Changing speed, direction, or duration recalculates projection length.

Zone selection

  • Enable zone selection, then click a complete zone to toggle it.
  • Selected zones use a solid selection fill.
  • Select All Zones applies to the current office/type and must be used cautiously.
  • Active product overlays are hidden during zone selection so they do not intercept clicks.

Marine zones

Special Marine Warnings can use a storm polygon or complete marine-zone selection. Optional motion affects wording/TML; it does not reshape selected marine zones.

8. Product-specific guidance

Tornado Warning (TOR)

  • Select a valid source and tornado status.
  • Use Considerable or Emergency impact levels only when supported.
  • Tornado Emergency wording requires the emergency location and exceptional life-threatening circumstances.
  • Review the TORNADO status, damage-threat, and maximum hail tags. TOR products do not use the SVR maximum-wind tag.

Severe Thunderstorm Warning (SVR)

  • Enter the maximum expected wind gust and hail size.
  • The hazard/source/impact text and coded tags must agree.
  • Destructive wording is reserved for qualifying threats.
  • SVR/TOR products are limited to 12 counties in the validation workflow.

Special Weather Statement (SPS)

Use an actual polygon for the affected area. SPS must not be displayed publicly as county-filled geometry simply because counties intersect it.

Special Marine Warning (SMW)

Select polygon or marine-zone geometry, source, hazard, motion, and affected waters. Confirm marine UGCs and avoid land county codes.

Fire Warning (FRW)

Identify the requesting agency, fire scenario, fire location/behavior, threatened locations, and any arrival time. Emergency wording should match verified urgency.

Winter/non-precipitation/coastal/fire-weather hazards

Zone templates expose product-specific amounts such as snow, ice, wind, temperature, visibility, water level, waves, surf, rip-current risk, humidity, and timing. The configured template determines where each field appears. Do not retain irrelevant values from another product.

9. Hazard criteria reference

Policy ownerOperations Administration RevisionAugust 7, 2026 VerificationReview before every issuance

Local-threshold warning: The numerical criteria below are taken from regional examples updated July 25, 2026. They are included to teach how criteria are structured—not as universal thresholds for RIW, BIS, CYS, OHX, or another office. Before issuance, apply the operated WFO’s current local policy and governing directive. Protection of life and property may justify action below a numerical threshold.

Confidence and product level

  • Watch: PQR guidance generally uses confidence of at least 50% that warning-level/impact conditions will occur, with uncertainty in timing, location, or occurrence.
  • Warning: PQR guidance generally uses confidence of at least 80%, or an event that is observed/imminent.
  • Advisory: Hazardous conditions are expected but generally below warning-level severity; meaningful disruption or danger is still possible.
  • Statement: Supplies updates, corrections, continuations, expirations, or cancellations for an existing product family.

Winter weather

The winter-weather operations standard controls the WSW product suite and format. PQR snow amounts vary sharply by elevation/zone; the operated office’s zone-specific thresholds must replace these examples. Criteria are guidance and impact-based issuance remains available with coordination and documented reasoning.

Wind, heat, cold, fog, smoke, ash, and dust

Product rules: The NPW uses zone UGCs and the “URGENT - WEATHER MESSAGE” broadcast line. Watches generally require at least 50% confidence and should not be newly issued within 12 hours of onset. Warnings and advisories generally require at least 80% confidence. Update active NPWs at least every 12 hours. Use FROM/TO when issuance is at least three hours before onset; otherwise use UNTIL. Follow the current non-precipitation operations standard.

Coastal and beach hazards

Coastal-product rules: CFW products use zone UGCs, no overview, and segmented WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, IMPACTS, optional ADDITIONAL DETAILS, and precautionary actions. Watches and warnings include “URGENT - IMMEDIATE BROADCAST REQUESTED”; advisories and statements do not. Issue updates at least every 6–8 hours. Watch UGC expiration is generally 12 hours; warning, advisory, and statement UGC expiration is generally 6–8 hours. Follow the current coastal operations standard.

Marine hazards

Marine Weather Statement rules: The marine-statement standard uses the “Marine Weather Statement” header with marine-zone UGCs and no MND broadcast line. General MWS events use a non-VTEC segmented format and expire no more than 12 hours after issuance. SMW continuations, cancellations, and expirations use MWS with MA.W VTEC and expire no more than 15 minutes after the warning end. LAT...LON and TIME...MOT...LOC are included for WarnGen polygons.

Marine Weather Message rules: MWW watches, warnings, and advisories cover complete marine zones and use URGENT - MARINE WEATHER MESSAGE, Z-form UGC, VTEC, and WHAT/WHERE/WHEN/IMPACTS bullets, optional ADDITIONAL DETAILS, and precautionary actions. Watches use “possible” in WHAT and FROM…THROUGH headlines; warnings/advisories use “expected” and FROM…TO or UNTIL. There is no overview or separate broadcast line. Follow the current marine-warning operations standard.

Fire weather

Fire-weather standard: National rules are combined with locally coordinated criteria in each office’s Annual Operating Plan (AOP). Red Flag criteria always combine fuel dryness with weather parameters; the standard does not establish one national RH or wind threshold. Do not use “explosive fire growth” in RFW text. Describe the specific weather parameters or extreme fire behavior instead.

Product/service Timing Operational rule and required content
Fire Weather Watch (FW.A/RFW) 18–96 hours before onset High potential—not merely low confidence or borderline conditions—for a locally defined Red Flag event. Use fire-weather zones or coordinated sub-zone portions. Include affected area, timing, no more than three weather-element bullets, fuel status, fire-behavior impacts, and a brief CTA.

11. Hydro operations

Flash Flood Warning

FFWs are polygon products using FF.W. Confirm the immediate cause, source, current/expected flooding, warning duration, emergency location when applicable, and polygon.

  • The first bullet defines the warning area/cause.
  • The second bullet gives the ending time.
  • The third bullet provides time and warning basis.
  • HAZARD, SOURCE, and IMPACT describe the threat.
  • LAT…LON is mandatory.
  • FLASH FLOOD source, damage threat, expected rainfall, and dam/levee tags appear when applicable.

Damage threat

Base
Normal FFW; no damage-threat tag.
CONSIDERABLE
Unusually severe, urgent life-safety threat.
CATASTROPHIC
Exceedingly rare, disastrous flooding. Requires Flash Flood Emergency language.

Dam and levee scenarios

Distinguish dam failure, imminent dam failure, occurring failure, levee failure, floodgate/reservoir release, ice jam, glacier-dammed lake outburst, rapid snowmelt, and volcanic/lahar scenarios. Enter the structure, river/drainage, downstream locations, failure state, reporting source, and site-specific impacts.

Areal Flood Warning

FA.W uses WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, IMPACTS, and ADDITIONAL DETAILS. Choose the flooding type and immediate cause. Follow-ups are issued as Flood Statements where appropriate.

Flood Advisory

FA.Y describes elevated flow or ponding below warning severity. Select the type, cause, source, ongoing/expected state, drainages, rainfall, impacts, and optional details. Minor flooding does not mean harmless flooding.

Automatic NWPS gauge import

  1. Enable River Gauges in Layers.
  2. Click a gauge marker and inspect its category, observation, and time.
  3. Select Use in Hazard Services.
  4. The system opens an Areal Flood Warning unless a Flood Advisory is already being edited.
  5. Review the gauge identifier, river/location, observed stage, official forecast crest/time, trend, category, action/minor/moderate/major thresholds, impacts, and forecast issuance time.
  6. Select the review-confirmation checkbox.
  7. Use Refresh Gauge immediately before final review if significant time has passed.

Gauge limitations

  • Some gauges provide observations but no official forecast.
  • Some gauges have no defined flood thresholds or impacts.
  • Values marked unavailable must not be invented.
  • A forecast crest is the maximum in the available official forecast series; confirm it against the hydrograph.
  • Imported values support authoring but do not replace hydrologic judgment or local procedures.

Forecast-point products

Forecast-point FLW/FLS products require gauge/gauge identifier identity, observed and forecast stage/flow, flood stage/category, crest, timing, and level-specific impacts. Gauge-specific values must be reviewed even when automatically imported.

12. Reviewing generated text

Regenerate after changes

Changing a form field after entering Review does not rewrite text that was already generated. Return to the applicable step or change the field, then select Review again.

Required checks

  1. WMO/AWIPS identifier and issuing office.
  2. UGC codes and expiration.
  3. VTEC action, office, phenomena, significance, ETN, start, and end.
  4. Headline and product name.
  5. WHAT/WHERE/WHEN or warning bullets.
  6. Hazard, source, impact, amounts, timing, and locations.
  7. LAT…LON/TML and coded tags.
  8. CTA wording and product terminator.

Placeholders

Text such as EDIT LOCATION, template markers, braces, or missing-value tokens blocks issuance. Return to Details and complete the required field rather than deleting an unresolved token from protected text.

Protected content

Headers, VTEC, UGC, geometry, TML, and coded tags are generated from state and are protected. Narrative text may be refined, but edits must remain consistent with selected fields and actual conditions.

13. Updates and follow-ups

Loading an active product

  1. Open the active warning/product list.
  2. Select the product and choose Edit/Update.
  3. Confirm the issuing office and acquire its edit lock.
  4. Review the existing geometry, expiration, and latest text before selecting an action.
CON — Continue
Keep the current expiration while updating current conditions, basis, locations, and impacts.
EXT — Extend in time
Set a later expiration because the hazard will persist. Verify all Until wording changes.
EXA/EXB
Expansion actions used only by products/workflows that support them.
CAN — Cancel
End the product before expiration when the threat has ended. Retain road-closure or residual-impact advice where appropriate.
EXP — Expire
Allow the product to end at its scheduled time and state that the threat no longer warrants continuation.

Corrections

Corrections address text/format errors only where permitted. Do not use COR to change the warned area, expiration, event number, or other values tied to VTEC. Use the appropriate lifecycle action or replacement product.

Partial-area changes

When a product family does not permit a direct area expansion/reduction, follow its required cancel/reissue or split-segment procedure. Never make the map look smaller while leaving UGC/VTEC coverage unchanged.

14. Issuance and distribution

Pre-issue checklist

  1. Confirm operational versus Practice Mode.
  2. Read the complete reviewed text.
  3. Verify office, action, ETN, start/end, UGCs, polygon/zones, tags, and CTAs.
  4. Confirm imported gauge or observational timestamps.
  5. Resolve every validation failure.

Issuing

Select Issue once. Do not repeatedly select it because the interface appears slow. Wait for the server response and distribution receipt.

Receipt destinations

  • Ops active product inventory.
  • Public map/site when applicable.
  • Mattermost notification when configured.
  • Placefile/warnings inventory.
  • GR warning server for supported warning products.

Not every product goes to every destination. Practice Mode intentionally suppresses external/public distribution.

If a destination fails

Preserve the receipt, check Data & Server, and determine whether the product itself was stored successfully. Do not create a duplicate event to compensate for a notification-only failure.

Rapid-update workflow

Convective operations

Readiness

  • Review outlooks, watches, observations, environmental analysis, and expected storm mode.
  • Load radar with useful neighboring sites, velocity, reflectivity, storm reports, boundaries, and warning layers.
  • Confirm warning inventory, office responsibility, Practice Mode status, and distribution health.

Warning cycle

  1. Interrogate multiple radar products and sources; confirm scan time and beam limitations.
  2. Choose the correct product and source, place the storm at the valid scan time, derive motion, and refine the downstream polygon.
  3. Confirm counties, locations, threat values, impact level, coded tags, expiration, and protective actions.
  4. Issue once, verify every destination, and immediately resume monitoring the warned storm.

Updates and closeout

Reassess on every meaningful scan. Continue, extend, replace, cancel, or expire using the supported lifecycle action. Record significant reports, preserve coordination notes, and hand off storm trends, active products, and next evaluation times.

Long-duration workflow

Winter operations

Readiness

  • Compare model runs, ensembles, observations, road temperatures, elevation effects, and precipitation-type transitions.
  • Identify local thresholds, vulnerable corridors, likely onset, peak, ending, and uncertainty.

Product cycle

  1. Choose watch, advisory, or warning based on expected impacts, confidence, and lead time.
  2. Select complete zones and review elevation-dependent amounts, ice, wind, visibility, and timing.
  3. Coordinate boundary timing and wording where adjacent offices or multiple hazards meet.
  4. Update amounts, impacts, and ending times as observations and forecasts converge.

Handoff

Document observed totals, precipitation type, road impacts, forecast changes, active segments, next update deadlines, and any zones near upgrade or cancellation.

Operations

Rainfall and river workflow

Flood and hydro operations

Monitoring

  • Track observed and forecast rainfall, radar estimates, soil conditions, reports, gauge trends, and known vulnerable basins.
  • Check gauge timestamps, flood categories, forecast availability, and upstream/downstream consistency.

Decision path

  1. Use Flash Flood Warning for rapid life-threatening flooding; use Areal Flood Warning or Flood Advisory for slower or lower-severity flooding; use forecast-point products for supported river locations.

  3. Use the correct lifecycle action for changes; do not create a second event to replace an update.

Watch desk

Select the watch type, number, duration, and threats; place and refine the watch box; review included offices and areas; then complete coordination before issuance. Confirm PDS status, hail/wind values, timing, box orientation, and the final area list.

Tropical forecast editor

Set storm identity first, then enter forecast positions in chronological order. Review valid times, status, intensity, coordinates, cone display, and labels. Save to the shared tropical desk only after checking for another operator’s newer work.

Display and feed controls

  • Radar color tables change presentation, not data values. Confirm the legend after every change.
  • Radar inventory shows product/frame availability; missing inventory is not proof of no weather.
  • Data & Server lists feed health and notifications. Record persistent failures and their operational impact.
  • Review the change log after deployments so interface or workflow changes are known before live operations.

21. Continuity, access, and recovery

Shift handoff

Record active products, saved drafts, edit locks, operated offices, data outages, coordination in progress, unresolved distribution failures, and the next required update times. The incoming operator should repeat the start-of-shift checks before accepting the desk.

Roles and account safety

  • Use only the permissions assigned to your role. Mutual Aid, template administration, audit access, and cross-office actions require explicit authorization.
  • Do not share accounts or leave an authenticated workstation unattended. Sign out at the end of the shift and after using a shared device.
  • If access is unexpectedly elevated, reduced, or assigned to the wrong office, stop affected actions and contact an administrator.

Outage and escalation

  1. Identify whether the failure affects the browser, application server, one data feed, authoring, storage, or distribution.
  2. Preserve error text, timestamps, product IDs, receipts, and screenshots. Avoid repeated issuance attempts.
  3. Use an approved alternate source or workstation when available and clearly mark stale data.
  4. Escalate through the configured operations contact with impact, start time, affected offices, actions attempted, and current workaround.
  5. After recovery, verify feed freshness, active-product inventory, locks, drafts, and every distribution destination before resuming normal work.

Backup, restore, and rollback

Before administrative template changes, preserve the current definition and record its owner and revision. Restore or roll back only through the approved administrative workflow, then reload the saved definition and test generation in Practice Mode. Never use a production issuance as a rollback test.

Practice drills and post-issue verification

  • Run drills in Practice Mode with a stated scenario, observer, expected result, and cleanup plan.
  • After issuance, verify the stored event, map geometry, text, timing, public display when applicable, and each receipt destination.
  • Document defects and near misses with enough detail to reproduce them; do not include unnecessary personal data.

23. Troubleshooting reference

Forbidden: access audit permission required
Confirm the user’s explicit audit permission/group and refresh the login session after permissions change.
Gauge unavailable
Retry Refresh Gauge, check NWPS health, and open the source hydrograph. Never silently substitute stale/manual data.
No gauge forecast
The station may be observation-only. Use only available official data and follow the applicable non-forecast workflow.
Red radar frame
The frame failed or is unavailable. Select a valid scan and inspect Data & Server.
Radar site error
Try another product/source/site, verify timestamps, and inspect feed health.
Form value missing from generated text
Regenerate Review. If still absent, verify that its field token exists in the applicable template section.
Template reload loses labels
Confirm the saved definition contains the full fields list and field-block tokens, not only default section prose.
Public alert missing
Check Practice Mode, expiration/action, map filters, and the distribution receipt.
Alert appears twice
Check whether two inventories/layers are enabled or duplicate active records exist; do not issue again until identified.
Wrong product on top
Review administrative layer priority and refresh both public views after saving.
Zone clicks do not work
Enable zone selection and confirm active-product hit layers are not intercepting input.
Issue button appears stuck
Wait for the receipt and inspect server status. Repeated clicks may create duplicate attempts.
Stored warning text not found
Confirm exact product ID, action, save state, and retention; the URL parameters are case/record specific.
Old content after an update
Refresh the page. If necessary, use a cache-bypassing reload after confirming the server is running the new version.
